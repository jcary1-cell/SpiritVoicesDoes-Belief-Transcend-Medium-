HISTORICAL ANCHOR • NEW YORK CITY, 1869

The Courtroom of Humbug & Devotion

Case: The People v. William H. Mumler
I

The Skeptics & P.T. Barnum

During the 7-day preliminary trial in NYC, showman P.T. Barnum testified against Mumler, declaring spirit photographs to be deliberate "humbug". Professional photographers demonstrated how double exposure darkroom tricks could easily duplicate the effect.

Courtroom Testimony (1869)

"Barnum testified he purchased Mumler's photographs to exhibit them in his museum as prime specimens of deception."

— American Museum of Photography
II

The Faithful & Moses Dow

Prominent figures like Moses A. Dow (Editor of Waverley Magazine) testified fiercely in Mumler's defense. For believers grieving lost loved ones after the Civil War, the photos provided genuine emotional solace that transcended darkroom mechanics.

The Believer's Defense

"To those sitting in darkness, the image was not a trick of chemistry, but a window into eternal affection."

— Testimony of Moses A. Dow
III

The Reluctant Dismissal

The magistrate reluctantly dropped charges due to insufficient legal proof of criminal fraud—leaving both sides claiming victory. Mumler was exposed yet exonerated, remaining financially ruined ($3,000 defense costs).

Judicial Decision

"However he might believe trick was practiced, the prosecution failed to legally prove criminal fraud."

— NYC Police Court, 1869

The Skeptic's Stance

Generative AI strips away human agency, dressing up algorithmic extraction as creative magic—much like Mumler's glass negatives dressed darkroom chemistry as ghostly presence.

The Practitioner's Stance

Prompting is a modern séance. By uttering text, creators channel latent frequencies to give voice to feelings that would otherwise remain silent inside them.
